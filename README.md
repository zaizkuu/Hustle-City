# Hustle City

A business life-simulation game for Android, inspired by *Big Ambitions*. Start with a small apartment, a beat-up car and a little cash. Rent a storefront, furnish it, stock it, hire staff, and grow a business empire in one of five living 3D cities: New York, Manila, Tokyo, Seoul or the original Hustle City grid.

Native Kotlin + Jetpack Compose with a custom 3D engine. In HD mode the world is drawn by an OpenGL ES 3 renderer with per-pixel lighting, real-time sun shadows, procedural materials (brick, stone, glass that reflects the sky, asphalt, sidewalk slabs, grass, water, wood, cloth, skin and hair) and 4x anti-aliasing; a lightweight Canvas renderer is the fallback for older devices.

Nearly everything you see and hear is generated by code at runtime: the cities, buildings, cars and people are modelled procedurally, and the music and sound effects are synthesized. The exceptions are the furniture, the products on store displays, the street furniture and the vehicles in HD mode, which are real 3D models: some converted from free (CC0) asset packs, the rest modelled for the game, all packed into a compact mesh format. With no textures or audio files to ship, the release APK is still under 3 MB.

## What's new in 1.8

- **Selling.** Sell a business as a going concern to a rival company (furniture and stock at a discount plus about two months of its recent profit), with or without the building. Sell a building and lease it back so your store or home stays put. Sell cars from the Persona app, wherever they're parked.
- **Being a landlord.** Offer a building you own to tenants in Realty, at a rent you set as a share of the market rent. Rival companies open real stores and offices in your shops; households rent your homes. Rent comes in nightly, leases renew every 60 days, and steep rents or big rises send tenants away.
- **Save slots.** Six slots with a Load game screen. Starting a new game or trying another city no longer overwrites the one you're playing. Older saves move into slot 1.
- **Local money.** Manila shows pesos (₱), Tokyo yen (¥) and Seoul won (₩). Prices step and round in local money; the economy underneath is the same in every city.
- **Balance pass.** Profit now grows with what a business costs to set up. Cheap shops pay back in a few days; supermarkets, fast food, hairdressers and nightclubs in about a week; gyms, cinemas and theaters in about nine days. Clothing, electronics and jewelry no longer outearn everything else.
- **People rebuilt (HD).** Smooth, jointed figures with faces, eight hairstyles and real outfits (suits and ties, dresses, hoodies, coats and scarves in the cold), joggers, dog walkers, parents with children, seniors with canes, and people who stop to read their phone or look in a shop window.
- **3D furniture models (HD).** Sofas, beds, fridges, TV stands, rugs, lamps, potted plants, desks with monitors, meeting tables, café table sets, barstools, filing cabinets, speakers, cash registers, shopping baskets and carts are now proper 3D models instead of stacked boxes. They turn with the piece, people sit on them, and the classic renderer keeps the old box models.
- **Real products on the shelves (HD).** Every product you can sell has its own 3D model: piles of apples, bananas, carrots and lettuce, rows of cans and bottles in the fridges, wine and whiskey, frozen pizzas, croissants and cupcakes, takeaway coffee, burgers, hot dogs, fries and pizza, popcorn, slushies, nachos and cotton candy, cocktails and pints, bouquets in zinc buckets, wrapped gifts, umbrellas, books, comics and magazines, T-shirts and blazers on hangers, sneakers, phones on stands, laptops, headphones, smartwatches, earbuds, ring boxes, necklaces and watches, and taped-up cardboard boxes in the stockroom. Clothing, books, gifts and other goods come in several looks, so a rail or a shelf isn't all one color. The models of full displays are kept on the GPU and only redrawn when the stock changes, so a packed supermarket costs no more per frame than the old box shapes.
- **Street furniture modelled (HD).** Cobra-head street lights on fluted posts, park lamps, fire hydrants with their outlets and caps, slatted litter bins, wood-and-iron benches, bus shelters with a bench and a stop sign, rows of newspaper boxes, blue mailboxes, bike racks with bicycles locked to them, concrete planters, mast-arm traffic signals, Manila's street food carts (fishball woks and snack cases under striped umbrellas) and concrete power poles (insulators, climbing rungs, transformers and junction boxes), Tokyo's vending machines (drinks on the shelves, lit from behind at night), basketball hoops and tiered park fountains are proper 3D models. Lamp lenses still light up at night, signals change color, shelters keep their glass and lit adverts, and benches gather snow. Props near the camera use the models and distant ones the simple shapes, so the city's cached geometry grows by under 1 MB.
- **3D vehicles (HD).** Sedans, hatchbacks, taxis with their roof signs, pickups, vans, sports cars, SUVs and box trucks are proper models: wheels in their arches, glass all round, grilles, bumpers, number plates and mirrors. So are each city's own: Manila's jeepneys (chrome horses on the hood, open windows between painted stripes, the route signboard on the roof rack) and modern jeepneys, tricycles with their covered sidecars, Tokyo's boxy two-tone kei cars, city buses with their doors on the curb side, motorbikes, and mopeds with a passenger riding pillion or a delivery box on the back. They take each vehicle's paint and its livery's stripes, lean on their springs through corners and under braking, and their headlights and tail lights light up after dark and in the rain, the brake lights when they slow; bus and jeepney windows light up at night. Traffic, parked cars, your own car and the dealer's showroom all use them. Further off, traffic switches to a light version of each model (plain wheels, flat lamps, no small parts) before the simple shapes, so cars driving away no longer turn into boxes halfway up the street; parked cars sit dark at night, and they're light enough that the city's cached geometry is smaller than before the models. The classic renderer keeps the simple shapes. Cars waiting to come back on the road no longer flicker where they last were.
- **Smarter traffic and pedestrians.** Cars stop behind the stop line instead of on the crossing, decide at amber whether they can stop, and the lights give a moment of red both ways so the junction clears. Turning cars wait for a gap in the oncoming traffic and for people crossing where they're turning into, cars move into the lane for their turn and overtake tricycles, buses and other slow traffic on bigger roads, and junctions only a few meters apart (the two halves of a divided road) work as one. People cross at the crossing, wait for a red long enough to get over, walk round the end of a car that's stopped across their way rather than through it, and go round the corner of the pavement instead of across the middle of a big junction. Jams where cars all wait on each other in a circle now clear themselves. Inside shops, customers step round each other and plan a way past people who are sitting, dancing or queueing instead of walking through them. Over five busy minutes per city, cars driving into people dropped by 83-99%, red lights run dropped by 77-98% (the original grid had almost none to begin with), cars passing through each other dropped in every city (least in Tokyo, where a few very sharp junctions still bunch cars up), and no car waits more than about a minute.
- **Tidier streets.** Every piece of street furniture is placed by its real footprint: nothing stands on anything else, in a doorway, in the road, under a parked car or in the water, and bike racks and bus shelters keep to the pavement. People on the sidewalks step round benches, shelters, trees, signal poles and stoops instead of walking through them (dog walkers give things a wider berth, and the dog falls in behind where it's narrow), and keep off pavements that run into the water. The paving now reaches the last streets at the edges of the map instead of leaving them, their lamps and their bus stops over open water, rivers run straight on off the edge, and streets that run past the shore end on concrete piers.
- **A furnished look.** Rooms dress their walls for their trade (bookcases, wine racks, TV walls, clothing rails, salon mirrors, menu boards, chalkboards, gym mirrors, office whiteboards, pictures at home), wood-tone floors are laid as planks, cars park along the curbs, and the pavements are busier.

## Features

**Five cities** (pick one when you start a new game)
- **New York**: Manhattan's tilted grid, Central Park, Broadway, Times Square, the Battery and Brooklyn across the East River.
- **Manila**: Manila Bay, the Pasig River, Intramuros with its walls and gates, Binondo, Quiapo and Makati's towers; jeepneys, tricycles and mopeds, sari-sari stores, food carts, jeepney terminals and power lines strung from pole to pole.
- **Tokyo**: the Imperial Palace and its moat, Shinjuku, Shibuya's scramble crossing, Ginza, Akihabara, Tokyo Tower and the Yamanote and Chuo lines. Cars keep left; vending machines line the side streets and red lanterns hang over the drinking alleys.
- **Seoul**: the Han River and its bridges, Gyeongbokgung and Gwanghwamun, Namsan Tower, Myeongdong and Gangnam, with hanok and pencil buildings.
- **Hustle City**: the original procedural grid of seven avenues, sixteen streets and six neighborhoods, in its classic layout or as a random city.
- Each city has its own street names, neighborhoods, landmarks, people's names and money.

**The city**
- Buildings with personality: brick walk-ups with fire escapes and water towers, brownstones with stoops and bay windows, art deco towers with setbacks and spires, glass skyscrapers, stone offices, modern blocks with balconies, industrial sheds, colonial houses, tropical homes, hanok and pencil buildings. Storefronts dress up for their business (striped awnings, neon, cinema marquees, window displays, sidewalk tables) and close their shutters at night.
- Street life: lamp posts that light the street, hydrants, benches, bus shelters, newspaper boxes, bike racks, planters and parked cars along the curbs; parks with fountains; trees that blossom in spring, turn red and gold in autumn and stand bare in winter.
- A day/night cycle with sun-tracking shadows, lit windows and glowing street lamps. Dynamic weather follows the seasons (the game starts in April): clear, cloudy, rain, thunderstorms, snow that settles on roofs and sidewalks, and fog. Puddles gather after rain, and the top bar shows a three-day forecast.
- Traffic with traffic lights: cars plan their turns, change lanes and overtake, keep their distance, lean on their springs when braking and turning, and give way to oncoming traffic and pedestrians. People cross at the crossings on the walk signal, step aside for each other and round stopped cars, walk into open shops, and crowds follow each district's day (office lunch rushes, nightlife evenings). Buildings turn see-through when they block your view.
- Walk with the on-screen joystick, drive your own car (fuel, condition and cargo space), or take a taxi from any building card.
- A minimap that turns with the camera, a full city map, and GPS navigation with a blue route line and turn-by-turn directions.

**Your character**
- Energy, hunger and happiness. Sleep at home, eat from your fridge or at restaurants, and avoid passing out.
- Homes are studios, apartments, condos or mansions; better homes mean better sleep and happiness.
- Courses at Hustle Business School unlock hiring, logistics, marketing, importing and real estate.

**Businesses** (21 types, the full Big Ambitions roster)
- Shops: Florist, Fruit & Vegetable Store, Coffee Shop, Supermarket, Fast Food, Hairdresser, Gym, Gift Shop, Liquor Store, Clothing, Bookstore, Electronics, Jewelry.
- Nightlife and shows: Nightclub (needs a DJ), Cinema (projectionist, licence fees) and Theater (actors, licence fees).
- Office firms: Law Firm, Web Development Agency, Graphic Designer, Travel Agency and Event Planning Agency. Professionals at desks bill clients by the hour; clients come from reputation and advertising.
- Rent or buy a building in **Realty** and pick a business type, comparing inventory sources, nearby competitors and primary products.
- Buy furniture at the appliance stores, load it into your car (or pay for delivery) and place it with the grid-based **Interior Designer**, with floor and wall colors. A full range for every trade: counters, baskets, security, trash cans, pizza ovens, ice cream freezers, scales, gym machines, coat checks, lounge sofas, stage lights, racks, mannequins, rugs, copiers, meeting tables and more.
- Buy wholesale stock at Metro Wholesale, CityDistro or Global Imports, then unload it onto shelves, fridges and machines; what doesn't fit is boxed in the back room and restocked by your staff.
- Customer simulation is driven by traffic, marketing, the hour, weather, competitors, price acceptance, wait times, cleanliness, interior and staff skill. Weather changes who shops and what they buy (umbrellas in the rain, ice cream in the heat, coffee in the cold).
- Customers track in dirt, spill things and drop litter; cleaners scrub the dirtiest spots first. Shoplifting hits jewelry, electronics, clothing and liquor stores unless cameras and gates stop it.

**Running an empire** (1.7)
- **Fleets**: buy a van or box truck, hire a delivery driver and plan standing routes in the **Fleet** app: load at a wholesaler or your warehouse at a set hour and drop stock at your stores in order. Delivery contracts can reorder automatically when a product runs low.
- **Staff**: morning, afternoon and graveyard shift presets; fatigue builds on long and night shifts, and tired staff serve slower, ring up sales wrong and miss restocks. Store managers keep a store staffed, look after unhappy and exhausted staff and run its reordering. Perks (free coffee, health plan, bonus scheme) and training courses.
- **Warehouses and headquarters**: fill pallet racks with bulk stock; HR Managers keep staff happy and replace anyone who quits, Purchasing Agents restock four stores each every morning.
- **Buyouts**: buy rival stores outright as subsidiaries that pay you their profit every night, or sell them on.
- **Leases**: leases renew every 60 days, and landlords raise the rent on busy, thriving spots.
- **Markets**: the **Markets** app trades shares in the city's firms and your rivals (own over half to take one over) and sells city bonds.

**Property** (1.8)
- Buy buildings once you've taken Real Estate Investing; sell them outright or lease them back.
- Let buildings you own to tenants: shops and offices to rival companies, homes to households. Set the asking rent, stop listing, or end a tenancy for a month's rent in compensation.
- Sell a business with its staff, stock and furniture to the company that bids most.

**Rivals**
- 8 AI companies, each with its own strategy (balanced, aggressive, premium, discounter, specialist). Their stores earn money with the same demand model as yours. They expand into good locations, renovate busy stores, advertise, buy their buildings, close money-losers, poach your best people and start price wars with your best stores.
- Bankruptcy after three midnights deep in debt (or keep playing in sandbox mode), and a victory screen when you're #1 with a $5M net worth.

**Sound**
- A phone radio with four procedurally generated stations (Lo-fi, Pop, Jazz, Electro), plus effects for purchases, the cash register, doors, notifications, rain, thunder and the car engine. Everything is synthesized at runtime.

**On your HustlePhone**
- **BizHub**: Insight, Inventory & Pricing, Schedule, Deliveries, Marketing and Settings (where you can also sell or close a business).
- **Staff**, **Finance** (daily profit chart, costs and Hudson Bank loans), **Realty** (rent, buy, sell, lease back, let to tenants), **Fleet**, **Markets**, **Rivals**, **Maps**, **Persona** (stats, education, home and vehicles, which you can sell from here) and **Contacts** (messages).
- Midnight settlement with a day summary and autosave into the current save slot. Guided objectives from Uncle Sal.

## Building

Requirements: JDK 17 or newer and the Android SDK (API 35).

```bash
./gradlew assembleDebug          # app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk
./gradlew assembleRelease        # minified, signed with the debug key for sideloading
./gradlew testDebugUnitTest      # simulation tests + offscreen render snapshots
```

Every push also builds both APKs on GitHub Actions. Download them from the run's **Artifacts** section.

The unit tests use Robolectric's native graphics to render the cities, interiors and every main UI screen to PNG files in `app/build/snapshots/`. `GpuFrameDumpTest` dumps real HD frames to `app/build/glframes/`, and `python3 tools/glview/render.py` renders them with WebGL2 in headless Chromium exactly as a phone would. `AudioTest` renders each radio station to WAV files in `app/build/audio/`. `BalanceReportTest` writes a per-business-type profit and payback report to `app/build/balance.txt`.

### 3D models

The HD renderer's models live in `app/src/main/assets/models/` (furniture), `app/src/main/assets/models/products/` (products) and `app/src/main/assets/models/props/` (street furniture) and `app/src/main/assets/models/cars/` (cars) as `.hcm` files (positions, packed normals, colors and shader surface per vertex, 16-bit indices). They're built from the glTF/GLB sources in `tools/models/sources/` and from shapes modelled in Python:

```bash
pip install numpy pillow
python3 tools/models/build_models.py --preview models.png   # rebuild all, plus a contact sheet
python3 tools/models/build_models.py sofa bed apples        # rebuild some
python3 tools/models/build_models.py --products             # only the products
python3 tools/models/build_models.py --props                # only the street furniture
python3 tools/models/build_models.py --cars                 # only the vehicles
```

`tools/models/manifest.py` says which source meshes make up each furniture id, which way they face and the box they're fitted into (in the item's tile footprint, front toward the customer). `tools/models/products.py` does the same for products, in meters at their real size: what one slot of a display shows (six cans, a pile of apples, a T-shirt on its hanger), with variants for products that come in several looks. `tools/models/props.py` models the street furniture and traffic signals, in meters around where each stands, facing the road; what glows or shows through is left to the game to draw on top. `tools/models/cars.py` models the vehicles, nose to +x on their wheels, in white or grey paint the game tints to each vehicle's color (and magenta for its livery's second color), with their lamps and windows as lit surfaces the game switches on and off; buses, modern jeepneys and tricycles come mirrored too, for cities where traffic keeps left, and every vehicle but the bikes has a light version for the middle distance (`<id>_lod`). Products and props no free pack has are modelled with `tools/models/shapes.py`, a small kit of boxes with rounded edges, lathed and extruded shapes, tori and tubes. Material names pick the shader surface (wood, cloth, glass, foliage, glossy). A furniture id without a model file keeps its box model; `ModelsTest` checks that every furniture model fits its footprint, that every product has a real-sized, light enough model, that the street props stay light and that every car is the size it's drawn at, with paint, headlights and tail lights, and `GpuFrameDumpTest` renders galleries of the products and props, a stocked store of each kind, close-ups of the props in the street and lines of every vehicle model by day and night.

## Project layout

```
app/src/main/java/com/hustlecity/game/
  data/      Catalog: products, furniture models, business types, vehicles, courses, places
  model/     Serializable save state
  world/     The five city maps, city generator, traffic, pedestrians and curbside parking
  render/    Projector, triangle batcher, GPU renderer, city / interior renderers, people, decor
  audio/     Procedural music stations, sound effects and the audio mixer
  engine/    Game loop, economy, rivals, operations, real estate, interiors, money, save slots
  ui/        Compose HUD, phone apps, BizHub, shops, menus
app/src/main/assets/models/   Furniture, product and street prop meshes for the HD renderer (built by tools/models)
tools/       Icon generator, GPU frame viewer, 3D model converter, trailer and cinematic tooling
```
